## Privacy Policy

This policy explains what personal data FunctionAlps collects, why we process it, who it reaches, how long we keep it and what you can require of us. Much of it is health data, which the law treats as especially sensitive and which we process only with your explicit consent. It covers the whole of FunctionAlps (the app, the members dashboard, our website and the emails we send you) because it is one service on several screens, run by one operator, holding one record about you.

### 1. At a glance

- We process what you log (meals and their photographs, check-ins, symptoms, supplements, measurements) to show you how your body responds to food. It makes no difference whether you logged it in the app or on the members dashboard: it is one record.
- Your meal photographs and meal descriptions are sent to OpenAI, in the United States, to identify the foods and estimate the portions, without your name or any identifier that would let OpenAI know they come from you (see section 8b). Everything else is analysed by Infomaniak, a Swiss provider, on infrastructure in Geneva, and is not sent to OpenAI, Google, Anthropic or any other general-purpose AI provider. None of your data is used to train anyone’s models.
- If you use voice input, the recording is transcribed by Infomaniak and then handled like anything else you log.
- If you connect Apple Health on your iPhone, we read the readings you allow (steps, sleep, heart rate and similar, including what your Apple Watch recorded) and nothing is written back. That is described in section 9a.
- If you link a wearable account (Oura, WHOOP, Polar, Garmin, Withings, Suunto or Google Fitbit), we receive the readings that service holds about you, under an access credential you can revoke at any time. That too is described in section 9a.
- If you took the self-assessment on our website, your answers are analysed the same way and we email you the result. That is described in section 8a.
- Your practitioner and the FunctionAlps care team can see your data, in order to support you. Nobody else can.
- We never sell your data, never share it with advertisers or insurers, and never use it for automated decisions with legal effect.
- You can see, export, correct or permanently delete everything at any time, and withdraw any consent you have given.
- We are in Switzerland and apply the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP) and, where it applies to you, the EU GDPR.

### 8. AI processing, profiling and automated decisions

Identifying the foods in a meal photograph or description, and estimating their portions, is performed by an AI model operated by OpenAI in the United States. The nutritional values of each recognised food come from our reference tables or are estimated by that same OpenAI model from the name of the food alone. Section 8b sets out both steps exactly. Meal scores follow fixed rules in our own code. Detecting patterns and drafting reports are performed by AI models operated by Infomaniak on infrastructure in Geneva, Switzerland.

This is profiling in the sense of the GDPR: we analyse your data to evaluate aspects of your health and habits. It does not, however, make decisions producing legal effects concerning you or similarly significantly affecting you within the meaning of Article 22 GDPR. Nothing in FunctionAlps decides anything about you; it describes and suggests, and every consequential decision remains yours or your practitioner’s.

We de-identify data before analysis: your name, email address and contact details are not attached to what is sent. We want to be straightforward with you about the limits of that, because they are real: a photograph may show your face, hands or home, and free text or a voice recording may contain a name, place or detail that identifies you. De-identification reduces the risk substantially; it does not make the data anonymous.

So we do not claim your data is anonymous. We continue to treat everything sent for analysis as your personal health data, with the same protections, retention periods and rights.

Your data is not used to train AI models, ours or anyone else’s.

Written interpretations in your periodic reports are reviewed by a qualified practitioner before you see them. Meal identification, nutrient estimates, scores and website assessment summaries are generated automatically and are not individually reviewed before you see them; we label automatically generated material where you see it.

### 8b. Meal photographs and OpenAI: exactly what is sent

Only one function of FunctionAlps uses a provider outside Europe: recognising the foods in the photograph or description of a meal. This is exactly how it works.

1. You photograph or describe a meal. Before it is sent, the app re-saves the photograph as a compressed image, which removes the metadata a photo file carries (location, date, device model). If you describe the meal out loud, the recording is transcribed by Infomaniak in Switzerland; the audio is never sent to OpenAI.
2. Our servers send OpenAI only: the meal photograph or photographs (four at most), the text of your description if you wrote or dictated one, and our analysis instructions. No name, email address, phone number, date of birth, account or member identifier, and nothing else from your record (check-ins, symptoms, readings, medical documents) goes with it. The request is made by our servers, in FunctionAlps’ name: OpenAI receives neither your IP address nor any way of knowing whose photograph it is.
3. OpenAI returns a simple list: the name of the dish, the foods it recognised, an estimate of their weight in grams, and a few preparation characteristics (for example fried or industrially processed). That answer contains no personal information.
4. We attach that list to your meal, in your record held in the European Union. Only on our side is the result linked to you.
5. For the nutritional values, only the name of each recognised food (for example "cooked brown rice") may be sent to OpenAI, with no photograph, no description and nothing about you. Where you have corrected a food, or if OpenAI does not answer, the values come from our reference tables.

**What OpenAI does with it.** OpenAI acts as our processor, on our instructions, under a data processing agreement. It does not use what it receives to train its models. It may keep it for up to 30 days, solely to detect abuse, and then deletes it. We do not ask it to store our exchanges.

**Why we do not say "anonymous".** A meal photograph can show, without your meaning it to, a face, a hand, a reflection, a room in your home or a document on the table, and a description can contain a name or a place. OpenAI receives no way of linking the photograph to you, but we cannot guarantee that its content will never let someone be recognised. So we treat it as personal data, and as health data because it is part of your nutrition follow-up, with every safeguard that goes with that. The safest habit: frame the plate and nothing else.

The safeguards for this transfer to the United States are described in section 12.

### 9a. Apple Health and wearables

If you choose to connect Apple Health in the app, the app reads the categories you allow in Apple’s permission sheet (including what your Apple Watch, or any other device that syncs into Apple Health, has recorded there) and sends them to your FunctionAlps record. It only reads: it never writes anything to Apple Health, and Apple is not a party to the transfer.

The readings are reduced on your phone before they are sent: one value per day for each category (your steps, your resting heart rate, how long you slept and in which stages, and so on) and one entry per workout. Those daily values are stored with the day they belong to and with a marker saying they came from Apple Health, and they are shown to you and to your practitioner next to your meals and check-ins, so that your body’s response can be read together with what you logged. They may be used in the scores and patterns the service computes for you, under the rules in section 8.

Wearable readings are health data and are covered by the explicit consent described in section 7. They are never sent to OpenAI, Google, Anthropic or any other general-purpose AI provider.

You can stop the connection at any time on the Devices screen in the app; the phone then stops sending. You can also withdraw the app’s permission for any category, or for everything, in Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices on your iPhone. Readings already sent stay in your record under the rules in section 13 and are deleted with everything else when you delete your account.

**Linked wearable accounts.** On the Devices screen you can also link an account you hold with Oura, WHOOP, Polar, Garmin, Withings, Suunto or Google (Fitbit). You are sent to that vendor’s own sign-in and consent page; what you allow there is what we receive. The vendor then sends us your readings (sleep, heart rate and heart-rate variability, activity and workouts, body measurements and, where the service computes them, its scores) either as they arrive or through a daily pull, and we store them with the day they belong to and the vendor they came from. To do this we keep an access credential for your vendor account, encrypted, on our servers; nobody reads it, it is used only to fetch your readings, and it is deleted the moment you unlink the account in the app, revoke it at the vendor, or delete your account. The vendor remains responsible for its own service under its own privacy policy; we do not send it anything about you. No aggregator or intermediary company is involved. Readings already received stay in your record under the rules in section 13. When you unlink, you can also choose to delete the readings that account had already sent: we then remove them from your record right away, and the raw copies we keep for traceability follow within a month. Every link, unlink and deletion is recorded in a log that you can ask us for at any time. Readings from a linked account serve only your own scores and your practitioner’s view; they are never passed to an artificial-intelligence service.

### 12. Where your data is processed, and transfer safeguards

Your data is stored and processed in the European Union and in Switzerland, with one exception: your meal photographs and meal descriptions are sent to OpenAI in the United States to identify the foods, under the safeguards described in the next paragraph. The European Union and Switzerland are each recognised by the other as providing an adequate level of data protection, and Switzerland is recognised as adequate by the European Commission. The web app’s files are delivered by Vercel’s global network, which sees only your connection data; everything you log is stored in the EU and Switzerland as described.

Where a provider or its parent is established outside Switzerland or the EEA, transfers are protected by appropriate safeguards: the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses, in the version recognised by the Swiss authorities, together with supplementary technical measures such as encryption in transit and at rest. That is the case for OpenAI. The United States is recognised as providing an adequate level of protection only for companies certified under the Data Privacy Framework (EU-US and Swiss-US), and OpenAI is not. The transfer of your meal photographs and descriptions therefore relies on the Standard Contractual Clauses in our data processing agreement with OpenAI, together with the measures described in section 8b: no identifier sent, retention limited to 30 days, no use for training. You can ask us for details of the safeguards applying to any specific provider.

### 13. How long we keep your data

We keep data only as long as it serves the purpose it was collected for:

- Account and profile data: while your account is active.
- Meals, check-ins, symptoms and supplements: while your account is active, so your own history and trends remain available to you.
- Voice dictation in the app (meals, check-ins, messages): the audio is never saved on our servers. It is sent to Infomaniak to be transcribed and discarded as soon as the text comes back; the text follows the entry it belongs to.
- Voice notes you leave in Analyses, on the members dashboard: the audio is kept with the note until you delete it or delete your account.
- Wearable readings: while your account is active, like the rest of your record; the raw submissions from your phone are kept alongside them so that a value can always be traced back to what the phone sent.
- Access credentials for linked wearable accounts: until you unlink the account, revoke it at the vendor, or delete your account; then deleted immediately.
- Raw copies of what a linked wearable account sent: 30 days after you unlink it, or right away when you ask us to delete those readings.
- Generated reports and interpretations: 12 months, or until you delete them.
- Website assessments and their summaries: 12 months from the day you took the assessment, or until you ask us to delete them, or until they are connected to an account, after which they follow that account.
- Messages: while your account is active.
- Technical and security logs: 12 months from each event, then deleted, including after your account is deleted. That matches the one-year minimum that Article 4 of the Swiss Data Protection Ordinance (DPO) sets for logging the processing of sensitive data.
- After you delete your account: all your personal data is erased within 30 days, except for the items listed below.
- Consent records: 10 years after your account is deleted, the 10-year limitation period of Swiss law (Articles 60 and 127 of the Code of Obligations), because they are the proof that we processed your data lawfully. They contain no health data.
- Accounting records, if you made a payment: 10 years from the end of the financial year concerned (Article 958f of the Code of Obligations). They contain no health data.
- The record your practitioner keeps: kept by them, separately from your account, for 20 years after your follow-up with them ends, the minimum set by Article 32 of the Valais Health Act, and are then destroyed.

### 16. Withdrawing consent, and what happens then

Withdrawal is always available and is never penalised beyond the direct consequence described here. You can withdraw in Privacy & Data, in the app or on the members dashboard, whichever you have open. It is deliberately no harder to withdraw a consent than it was to give it.

Withdrawing consent for news and offers, or for usage statistics, stops that specific processing and changes nothing else. The app continues exactly as before.

Withdrawing consent for health-data processing switches FunctionAlps off: the app and the members dashboard together, because it is one consent for one service. This is not a penalty: that processing is what the service consists of, and without it there is nothing we can do for you. Your data is not deleted by withdrawing: you can restore access by giving consent again, or delete your account to erase it.

The Terms of Service are not a consent and cannot be "withdrawn"; if you no longer accept them, stop using FunctionAlps and delete your account.
