*Functional health is how well your body works for you day to day, and over the next 14 days we build it from where you are, one small step at a time.*

Most of us only think about health when something goes wrong. But health is also the ordinary things: waking up with energy, a meal that sits well, a calm head in the afternoon, a body that moves without complaint, sleep that actually rests you. That is what we mean by functional health, and it is what this track is about.

## What functional health is

Functional health looks at how your body is working, not only at whether a test comes back normal. Two people can have the same results on paper and feel completely different in daily life. One sleeps well and has steady energy; the other is tired by mid-afternoon, bloated after meals and wired at night.

Your body is one system. What you eat affects your energy and your sleep. How you sleep affects your hunger and your mood. Stress shows up in your digestion. Movement helps almost everything. So instead of treating each problem on its own, we look at the patterns that connect them, and we work on the foundations underneath.

## The four pillars

Everything in this track rests on four pillars. You will meet one each day this first week.

1. **Nutrition** (day 2). Not a diet. How you eat matters as much as what you eat: slowly, without a screen, with real food on the plate.
2. **Movement** (day 3). Not sport. The everyday capacity to get up, bend, carry and climb. Some activity is better than none, and every bit counts.
3. **Sleep** (day 4). The time your body uses to recover. Sleep experts recommend that most adults sleep seven hours or more on a regular basis. Light, caffeine and your evening all shape how well you sleep.
4. **Mental and emotional health** (day 5). Breathing, attention and connection. Your mood is not separate from your body; research links movement, sleep and other daily habits with mental well-being.

The pillars support each other. A walk after lunch helps your energy in the afternoon; a calmer evening helps your sleep; better sleep makes the next day's choices easier.

## Why we start from where you are

There is no ideal starting point. A plan built for an imaginary person rarely fits a real one. So before we change much, we want to see your real pattern: what you eat, how your days feel, how you sleep.

That is why the first two habits are simply about noticing. Studies of goal-setting find that keeping track of your progress makes you more likely to reach your goals, especially when you actually record it. Tracking is not about control, and it should never make you obsessive. It should make you clearer. One bad day tells us little; a trend over two weeks tells us a lot.

## How the 14 days work

- **One short step a day.** Each day brings a theme and, most days, one or two new habits. They are small on purpose: a few minutes, never a new hour in your diary.
- **Your evening check-in.** One minute to rate how the day landed. After three days in a row, your curves appear in Trends. See *Your functional check-in, explained*.
- **Your meal photos.** A quick photo before the first bite. No counting. See *What your meal photos tell us*.
- **Two pauses.** Day 6 looks back on your first week. Day 13 is where you choose the habits you keep.
- **Your review.** On day 14 we look at your two weeks together, with Thomas.

If you miss a day, simply carry on the next one. Consistency beats perfection.

If you have a medical condition, are pregnant or take regular medication, ask your doctor or your nutritionist before changing your routine.

## What to do

Today, two habits.

**Log a meal**
- Open Log a meal, point, shoot.
- Before the first bite, not after.
- Drinks and snacks count.

**Do your functional check-in**
- Five sliders: energy, sleep, mood, calmness, digestion.
- Around the same time each evening.
- Honest beats perfect.

## What to look for

Over the next few days, many people notice that:

- they start to see their real pattern, without counting;
- they notice what keeps them full;
- their own patterns begin to show, and their curves appear in Trends.

**Functional health starts with seeing clearly where you are; small steps, repeated, do the rest.**
