Your whole day in four moments, morning to evening, and how each habit makes the next one easier.

Over the first week you added habits one pillar at a time: nutrition, movement, sleep, then mind. Seen one by one, they can feel like a long list. Seen as a day, they are something simpler: a few small moments, each tied to something you already do. Today you also make two upgrades: the 5-minute morning flow, and an exercise snack every day.

## Morning: wake the body, set the clock

- **Daylight first thing: head out of the window, and breathe.** Right when you wake up, before your phone.
- **5-minute morning flow.** New today. Five minutes inspired by qi gong: breathe with the arms, twist, shake it out. It takes over from your 3 minutes of movement.

Morning light helps set your body clock for the day, which is part of what makes you sleepy at the right time tonight. The flow loosens the body after the night and gives you a first win before the day gets busy.

## Midday (10:00–14:00): eat calmly, then move

- **Mindful eating: no phone, chew, breathe, a glass of water.**
- **A 5-minute walk after eating.** Right after the meal, or within half an hour.
- **5 minutes of breathing, once a day.** Sitting or lying down.
- **One exercise snack a day.** New today, and no longer optional: 10 squats, a minute of stairs, or 10 wall push-ups.

A calm meal helps digestion. A short walk afterwards helps your muscles use the energy from the meal, which can soften the energy dip that often follows. In one study of people with type 2 diabetes, a 10-minute walk after each meal lowered the rise in blood sugar after eating more than one 30-minute walk at another time of day. The exercise snack adds a short burst of effort. In a large study of people who didn't exercise, those who did a few vigorous bursts of 1 to 2 minutes in their everyday life had a lower risk of dying early. That kind of study shows a link, not proof, but it fits with what we see: a little effort, often, is worth having.

## Afternoon: protect tonight's sleep

- **No caffeine after 16:00.** Decaf, herbal tea or water after that.

Caffeine stays in the body for hours. Stopping by 16:00 gives it time to wear off before bed. If you are tempted to reach for a coffee in the afternoon, notice whether the midday walk and a steadier lunch make that dip smaller.

## Evening: slow down

- **The 3-2-1 rule: 3 h no food, 2 h no intense exercise, 1 h no screens.**
- **2 minutes of gentle stretching before bed.**
- **Write down three good things about your day.**
- **Optional: a mini breath before sleep.** In bed, lights off.

Together, these tell your body the day is over: digestion has finished, the heart rate has settled, the mind has a good thought to end on.

## How the habits link up

The day works as a loop. Morning light and no late caffeine help you fall asleep. Better sleep tends to mean more energy and fewer cravings tomorrow. More energy makes the morning flow and the exercise snack easier. Moving after meals helps keep energy steadier through the afternoon, so you need less caffeine. A calmer midday makes for a calmer evening.

Each habit is also tied to a moment you already have: waking up, a meal, bedtime. You don't need to remember a list. You need to notice the moment.

## What to do

**5-minute morning flow**
- Slow breathing with the arms
- Gentle twists, left and right
- Shake it out

**One exercise snack a day**
- 10 squats, a minute of stairs, or 10 wall push-ups
- Once a day, whenever suits you
- Easier is always fine

Move gently and stop if anything hurts. If you have a heart, joint or balance problem, ask your doctor before adding more effort.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- A loose, awake body and a better mood in the morning.
- Clarity of mind and more energy after an exercise snack.
- That one good moment makes the next one easier.

**Four moments, each tied to something you already do: that is your whole day.**
