*A few easy minutes on your feet after a meal help your muscles use the energy you have just eaten, and can soften the afternoon dip.*

You know the feeling: lunch is over, you sit back down, and an hour later your eyelids are heavy. A short walk after eating is one of the simplest ways to change that. No kit, no sweat, five minutes.

## What happens after a meal

When you eat, the carbohydrates in your meal are broken down into glucose (sugar) that enters your blood. That is normal and useful: it is fuel. Your body then works to move it out of the blood and into your cells.

Your muscles are the biggest user of that glucose. When they work, even gently, they take up more of it. Think of them as a sponge: the meal brings the fuel, and the walk switches on the tissue that can use it.

This is not about fearing carbohydrates. It is simply about helping your body handle a meal more smoothly.

## What the research shows

Studies that break up long periods of sitting with short bouts of light walking find lower blood sugar and insulin after meals than when people stay seated, and walking does better than simply standing. In one trial in people with type 2 diabetes, walking for 10 minutes after each main meal lowered blood sugar after meals more than one 30-minute walk at another time of day. The effect was largest after the evening meal.

For digestion, the evidence is smaller. One study in healthy volunteers found that a walk after a meal helped the stomach empty faster. Many people also find they feel less heavy, but your own feedback matters most here.

Five minutes is our starting dose. Some of the studies used longer walks, so think of today as the first step; on day 8 it grows, in *The walk after every meal*.

## Fitting it in, even at work

- **Right after eating, or within half an hour.** Whatever is comfortable.
- **Outside if you can.** Daylight and fresh air are a bonus.
- **No outside? The corridor works too.** Take the stairs, walk to the far coffee machine, take a call on your feet.
- **Easy pace.** You should be able to chat. This is not a workout.

If you have a medical condition, check with your doctor before changing your routine. If walking causes discomfort, slow down or wait a little longer after the meal.

## What to do

**A 5-minute walk after eating**
- Right after the meal, or within half an hour.
- Outside if you can; the corridor works too.
- Easy pace, no sweat.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- steadier energy after meals;
- easier digestion;
- less of the afternoon dip.

**Five easy minutes on your feet after eating help your body make good use of the meal.**
