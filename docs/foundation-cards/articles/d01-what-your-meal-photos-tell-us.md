*A quick photo before the first bite shows us your real eating pattern, with no counting and no weighing.*

Most of us don't really know what we eat. Not because we hide it, but because memory is kind to us. The handful of nuts, the second coffee, the biscuit at four o'clock: they disappear by the evening. A photo doesn't forget.

## Why photos, not counting

Counting calories or weighing food takes time, and for many people it turns eating into a test. That is not what we want. A photo takes two seconds, and it keeps the meal as it really was.

Research on food records shows that adding pictures to a food diary helps people report what they eat more completely; people tend to under-report less when they snap their meals. And studies of people changing their eating habits keep finding the same thing: those who keep a record of what they eat tend to do better, although the quality of that evidence is modest.

## What we look at

We are not judging your plate. We look for patterns over days, such as:

- **Colour and variety.** How many different plants appear across the week.
- **Protein.** Whether each meal has a source of it.
- **The kind of carbohydrate.** Whole grains, beans and fruit, or mostly white bread, pastries and sugary drinks.
- **Rhythm.** When you eat, how long the gaps are, whether meals get skipped.
- **The in-betweens.** Drinks, snacks and grazing, which often tell us the most.

Put next to your evening check-in, this helps us see what keeps you full and steady, and what leaves you tired or bloated.

## How to snap fast

- **Before the first bite.** Afterwards the plate is half-eaten, and it is easy to forget.
- **The whole plate, from above,** in reasonable light. It doesn't need to look good.
- **Drinks and snacks too.** A coffee, a glass of juice, an apple: they all count.
- **Missed one?** No problem. Carry on with the next meal.

## What to do

**Log a meal**
- Open Log a meal, point, shoot.
- Before the first bite, not after.
- Drinks and snacks count.

## What to look for

Many people notice that:

- they see their real pattern, without counting;
- their nutritionist sees what they eat, so advice fits their real life;
- they start to notice what keeps them full.

**A photo before the first bite is the easiest honest food diary there is.**
