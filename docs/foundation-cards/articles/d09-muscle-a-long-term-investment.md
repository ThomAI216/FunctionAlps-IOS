*Your muscle is a health account you pay into for life, and small, regular deposits count.*

Most people think of muscle as something for the gym, or for how you look. In daily life it is much more ordinary than that. It carries the shopping up the stairs. It gets you out of a low chair. It catches you when you trip on the pavement. It is also one of the busiest tissues for your metabolism, working quietly after every meal.

A helpful way to see it: muscle is an account. What you pay in now is what you draw on later.

## Why think of muscle as an account

Muscle responds to use. When you use it, your body keeps it, and can even build more. When you leave it unused, your body slowly lets it go. This happens so gradually that most people only notice when something that used to be easy, like the stairs or getting up from the floor, starts to feel hard.

Doctors have a name for serious loss of muscle strength and mass: sarcopenia. The European consensus on it describes changes that build up across a whole lifetime, not only in old age, and puts low *strength* at the centre, ahead of size. So the aim is not big muscles. It is muscles that work.

The good news is that the account never closes. A position statement from the National Strength and Conditioning Association concludes that strength training helps people keep strength, mobility and independence well into older age. It is never too late to start paying in.

## What your account pays for

**Your joints.** Muscles around a joint share the load and steady the movement. Strong thighs and hips support the knees; a strong back and middle support the spine. Many people find their joints feel more comfortable as the muscles around them get stronger.

**Your blood sugar.** Muscle is one of the main places your body sends sugar from a meal. Working muscle draws sugar out of the blood. In a large US survey of more than 13,000 adults, people with more muscle relative to their body size had better insulin sensitivity (how well the body handles sugar) and less prediabetes. That is a link, not proof of cause, but it fits what we know about how muscle works.

**Your independence.** Getting up from the floor, carrying a child or a suitcase, keeping your balance on uneven ground: all of these draw on strength. In a review of large population studies, adults who did muscle-strengthening activities had a 10 to 17% lower risk of early death, heart disease, diabetes and cancer. Again, these studies show a link, and people who do strength work may differ in other ways too. But the direction is consistent.

## Movement moments are the deposits

You do not need a gym to pay in. Every squat, every calf raise, every push-up against the wall is a small deposit. On their own they look modest. Repeated, day after day, they add up.

Early on, much of what changes is not the muscle itself but your nervous system learning to use it. As the library article *Strength Is More Than Muscle Size* explains, strength is what your muscles and nervous system produce together, and practice improves coordination and control. That is why simple moves feel easier within a couple of weeks.

Two small tips make each deposit count. Lower yourself slowly: the lowering part of a squat or calf raise is real work, not a rest. And give your body the building material, with protein at every meal (see *Protein at every meal, and why it matters*).

## What to do

Your habit from today: **2 to 3 movement moments a day**. It can be as light as a walk.

- Choose a movement: it can be as light as a walk.
- Or a few easy moves: squats, calf raises, a chest opener.
- Push-ups too, if you're comfortable.

Stop if anything hurts. If you have a joint problem, a heart condition or are recovering from an injury, ask your doctor or your nutritionist first.

## What to look for

Over the next days and weeks, many people notice:

- Clarity of mind after a movement moment.
- Stable blood sugar, which you may feel as fewer dips after meals.
- More energy through the day.
- A stronger body overall: stairs, bags and getting up from a chair start to feel easier.

**Every movement moment is a deposit in the account you will draw on for the rest of your life.**
