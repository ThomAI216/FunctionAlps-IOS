Writing down three good things about your day each evening gently trains your attention towards what goes well.

At the end of a day, the mind tends to replay what went wrong: the tense meeting, the thing you forgot, the to-do list for tomorrow. That's normal. Our brains are built to spot problems. But if that is the last thing you think about before sleep, it colours your evening and often your night. This small habit gives your mind something else to hold on to.

## Why it works

What you pay attention to shapes how you feel. Most good moments in a day are small and pass quickly: a kind word, a good coffee, sun on your face, a task finished. If you don't stop to notice them, they are gone by the evening.

Writing them down does three things. It makes you look for them. It makes you remember them. And over time, it trains you to spot them as they happen, not only at night.

## What the research shows

"Three good things" is one of the best-known exercises in positive psychology. In a large online trial, people wrote down three things that went well each day, and why, for one week. It was one of the exercises that raised happiness and eased symptoms of low mood, and the effects lasted for months.

Later reviews are more modest. A meta-analysis of gratitude exercises found they improve wellbeing a little, on average. That is an honest picture: it is a small daily tool, not a cure. But small and daily is exactly what this track is built on. There is also a link with sleep: people who feel more grateful tend to have calmer, more positive thoughts as they fall asleep, and sleep better.

## What to do

The habit is **Write down three good things about your day**.

- In the evening, in your phone or a journal.
- Small things count.
- One line on why it was good.

A paper journal by the bed works well, and it keeps you off the screen in your last hour. If you use your phone, write your three lines, then put it away.

Some evenings it will feel hard to find three. That's fine. "I had a quiet lunch" counts. Later in the track, *The habit of writing* builds on this with a few more minutes for what's on your mind.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- Falling asleep on a good thought.
- Over weeks, a more positive outlook.
- Noticing what helps you.

**Three small lines each evening teach your mind to notice what goes well.**
