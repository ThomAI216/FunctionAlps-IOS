Three simple time limits before bed, for food, hard exercise and screens, that give your body the quiet it needs to fall asleep.

Falling asleep is not a switch. Your body slows down step by step through the evening: your temperature drops, your heart rate settles, your mind quietens. The 3-2-1 rule simply keeps three things out of the way of that slowdown. It is easy to remember, and you can count it back from your usual bedtime.

## 3 hours: your last food

Digestion is work. A big meal just before bed keeps your gut busy at the time your body wants to rest. Lying down with a full stomach also makes it easier for acid to come back up, which can mean heartburn and broken sleep. In one study from Japan, people with reflux were far more likely than others to go to bed within three hours of dinner.

Finishing your last food about three hours before bed gives your stomach time to empty before you lie down.

## 2 hours: no hard exercise

Exercise in the evening is not the enemy. Overall, studies show it does not harm sleep for most people. The exception is hard, intense effort finishing close to bedtime, which can leave you hot, wired and slower to drop off. Two hours is a comfortable margin to cool down.

Gentle movement is different. Stretching, light movement and a walk are fine, and often help. Your 2 minutes of gentle stretching before bed fits right in.

## 1 hour: no scrolling

Screens do two things at night. Their bright light close to your eyes can delay your body clock, and their content keeps your mind switched on: one more video, one more post, one more email. Using the phone in bed is linked with taking longer to fall asleep and feeling more tired the next day.

This is not about never touching your phone. Answering a message is fine. It's the stimulating stuff you leave: scrolling, news, games, work.

## What to do

The habit is **The 3-2-1 rule: 3 h no food, 2 h no intense exercise, 1 h no screens**.

- 3 hours before bed: your last food of the day.
- 2 hours before: no hard exercise. Stretching, light movement and a walk are fine.
- 1 hour before: no scrolling. Answering a message is fine; it's the stimulating stuff you leave.

If all three feel like a lot, start with the one that is easiest for you, then add the others.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- Falling asleep faster.
- Waking less in the night.
- Calmer evenings.

**Three limits, counted back from bedtime: give your body a quiet runway into sleep.**
