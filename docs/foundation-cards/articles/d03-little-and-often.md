*Movement for health is about keeping your body capable, not about sport, and a few minutes you actually do each day beat a big plan you don't keep.*

Many of us have started an ambitious exercise plan in January and quietly dropped it by February. The plan wasn't wrong. It was just too big for a real life. Today, for the movement pillar, we go the other way: tiny, daily, easy to keep.

## Functional capacity, not sport

You don't need to be sporty to move well. What we care about is functional capacity: your body's ability to do what daily life asks of it. Getting up from a chair without your hands. Carrying the shopping up the stairs. Turning your head to reverse the car. Bending down to tie a shoe, and getting back up.

These abilities fade quietly when we sit most of the day, and they come back with regular, gentle use. Joints like to move through their range. Muscles like to be asked to work. Blood flow and breathing pick up the moment you get going. None of that needs a gym.

## Why small counts

For a long time, advice said exercise only "counted" in blocks of ten minutes or more. That has changed. A review carried out for the US physical activity guidelines found that activity of any bout length was linked to better health, including a longer life. The World Health Organization's 2020 guidelines say it plainly: some physical activity is better than none, and more is better still.

So your three minutes in the morning are not a token gesture. They are real movement, and they add up across the week.

## Why small sticks

There is a second reason to start small, and it may matter even more: habits. Research on habit formation shows that behaviours become automatic when they are repeated in the same situation, at the same moment of the day. The behaviour itself doesn't need to be big. What matters is that it is easy enough to do on your worst day.

Three minutes after you get up, two minutes before bed. Short enough that "I don't have time" stops being true. Once the habit exists, it is much easier to grow it, and over the coming days we will.

## Two moments: morning and evening

**In the morning**, three minutes to wake the body. Gentle circles of the neck, shoulders and hips loosen what stiffened overnight. Ten slow squats wake up the biggest muscles you have. Reaching up and folding down, with a breath, gets blood moving and gives you a first win before the day starts.

**In the evening**, two minutes of gentle stretching before bed. Slow and calm, with a long breath out each time. This is not about flexibility goals. It is a way to release the day's sitting from your hips, the backs of your legs and your chest, and a clear signal that the day is ending. Many people find it helps them settle for sleep.

Movement should never hurt. Stretch only to a gentle pull, never into pain, and stop if anything hurts. If you have a joint problem, an injury or a heart condition, check with your doctor or physiotherapist first.

## What to do

**3 minutes of movement** (morning)
- Neck, shoulder and hip circles.
- 10 slow squats.
- Reach up, fold down, breathe.

**2 minutes of gentle stretching before bed** (evening)
- Slow, never into pain.
- Hamstrings, hips, an open chest.
- A long breath out with each stretch.

## What to look for

Over the next days, many people notice:

- less stiffness in the morning, and feeling more awake, sooner;
- a first win in the day;
- looser back and hips, and an easier time falling asleep;
- a clear end to the day.

**Five minutes a day you actually do will take you further than an hour you keep postponing.**
