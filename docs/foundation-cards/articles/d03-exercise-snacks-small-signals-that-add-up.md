*An exercise snack is a short burst of effort, such as 10 squats or a minute up the stairs, and several of them across the day add up.*

A workout doesn't have to be one long block. Your body responds to the movement you accumulate over the day and the week, not only to what happens in a gym. An exercise snack is the smallest useful piece of that.

## What an exercise snack is

Researchers describe exercise snacks as short bursts of effort, often a minute or less, done a few times across the day. Not random movement, but a small, deliberate dose:

- 10 squats, up from a chair and back down;
- a minute up the stairs, at a pace that gets you breathing;
- 10 push-ups against a wall.

The best time is after you have been sitting a while. It breaks the sitting, and it wakes you up.

## Different snacks, different signals

Not every snack does the same thing.

- **Heart and breath snacks**, like stairs or a brisk uphill walk, mainly challenge your heart, lungs and circulation.
- **Strength snacks**, like squats or wall push-ups, mainly ask your muscles to work against resistance.

Both are useful. Pick the one that suits the moment.

## Why short bursts add up

The evidence is young but encouraging. Reviews of the trials so far find exercise snacks are feasible and appear safe for adults, including older adults, and can improve fitness, especially in people who were inactive. In a large study using activity trackers, people who did no formal exercise but had a few short, vigorous bursts in their daily life, such as climbing stairs or walking briskly, had a lower risk of dying over the following years. That kind of study shows a link, not proof of cause, and the researchers are still testing how far the benefits go.

What it does show: short does not mean meaningless.

## What to do

**Optional: an exercise snack**
- 10 squats, or a minute up the stairs.
- Or 10 push-ups against a wall.
- Any time you've been sitting a while.

Today this is optional. On day 7 it becomes *One exercise snack a day*.

Go at your own pace, and stop if it hurts. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, joint pain or are pregnant, check with your doctor before adding vigorous bursts.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- clarity of mind;
- more energy;
- steadier blood sugar (you won't feel this one directly, and the evidence so far comes from small studies);
- over time, a stronger body overall.

**Short does not mean meaningless: a focused burst of effort is a real signal, and small signals add up.**
