Five minutes of breathing a day is less about the perfect technique and more about giving yourself time, and training a calmer, more positive frame of mind.

Most days are full from the moment you wake up. Messages, work, family, errands: there is always something asking for your attention. It is easy to go a whole day without one moment that is just for you. Today's habit gives you that moment: five minutes, once a day, with nothing to do but breathe.

## Why your breath is a good place to start

Breathing is one of the few things in your body that runs on its own and that you can also take over at will. You can't tell your heart to slow down or your stress to settle. But you can change how you breathe, and your body receives a different signal.

Your state changes your breathing: when you are stressed, it tends to get fast and shallow. It works the other way too. Changing your breathing on purpose can change how you feel. That makes the breath a simple, free tool you carry everywhere.

## The practice we offer

To start, we offer a round inspired by Wim Hof, with a guided video, *Guided breathing, with Wim Hof*. A round looks like this: a series of deep, full breaths, then a slow breath out, then a pause, holding only as long as is comfortable. Breathe normally for a moment between rounds. Two rounds are enough to start.

Many people like it because it is lively. You will often feel tingling, warmth or a light head during the deep breaths. That is common with fast, deep breathing, and it passes when you breathe normally again.

To be honest about the evidence: research on Wim Hof-style breathing is still limited. A 2024 systematic review found only eight small trials. Some suggest effects on the body's inflammation response, but several of those studies combined the breathing with cold exposure and meditation, and results on other measures were mixed. Wider research on breathwork in general is more encouraging: a review of randomised trials found it was linked with lower stress, anxiety and low mood, while its authors warned against the hype.

## It's not about the technique

Here is the most important part. The exact method matters less than you might think. In a one-month study, people who did five minutes of structured breathing every day reported a better mood. Three different breathing styles were tested, and a calm, exhale-focused one did especially well. What they had in common was a short, regular moment of attention to the breath.

So if the Wim Hof round doesn't suit you, slow breathing with a long breath out is just as good a choice. Later in the track, in your evening routine, you will meet box breathing too.

What really counts is the time you give yourself. Five minutes that say: "I matter enough to stop."

## A more positive attitude, one moment at a time

A positive attitude is not about pretending everything is fine. It's about where your attention goes, and attention can be trained, a little like a muscle.

A few ways to use your five minutes for that:

- **Arrive.** Before you start, notice how you feel, without judging it. Tired, tense, flat: all are fine.
- **Breathe.** Do your rounds. Let the busy thoughts come and go.
- **Close with one kind thought.** One thing you are looking forward to, or one thing that went well. Then go back to your day.

Done once, it is a pleasant pause. Done daily, many people find their default mood shifts: a little more patience, a little less rush, a quicker return to calm after a hard moment. Tonight's habit, *Write down three good things about your day*, works the same way from the other end of the day.

## Safety first

Sit or lie down. Never practise in water, while driving or while standing. If you are pregnant, or have heart problems or epilepsy, skip this practice unless your doctor agrees. If you feel dizzy or unwell, stop and breathe normally.

## What to do

The habit is **5 minutes of breathing, once a day**.

- Sit or lie down; never in water or while driving.
- 30 deep breaths, then breathe out slowly.
- Hold as long as is comfortable; 2 rounds to start.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- A calmer, clearer head.
- Energy without coffee.
- A moment that is yours.

**The technique is just the door: the real practice is giving yourself five minutes, every day.**
