*NSDR is a guided way to rest deeply while you stay awake: a reset for your nervous system, not a substitute for sleep.*

Some evenings your body is tired but your mind will not stop. You lie down and the day replays itself: the email you forgot, tomorrow's meeting. Or in the middle of the afternoon you feel flat, but a nap would leave you groggy. NSDR is made for those moments. It gives your body a structured way to come down from "on" without having to fall asleep.

## What NSDR is

NSDR stands for *non-sleep deep rest*. It is a recent umbrella name for guided practices that help you relax deeply while staying awake, or close to it. The best known of these is **yoga nidra**, a traditional practice whose name means roughly "yogic sleep".

A session is simple. You lie on your back, close your eyes and follow a voice. It guides you to breathe slowly, then to move your attention through your body, part by part: your feet, your legs, your hands, your face. Sometimes it adds a picture to rest your mind on. You do not need to be flexible, and you do not need any experience with meditation. Your only job is to listen.

## How it differs from sleep

In sleep, you lose awareness and your brain moves through its own cycles across the night. These cycles do essential work for your memory, your mood, your hormones and your recovery, and nothing else does that work for you.

In NSDR, you stay aware, or drift at the edge of sleep. You may feel heavy, warm and very still. It can leave you feeling rested, but it **does not replace sleep**. Think of it as a way to help you towards sleep in the evening, or to recover a little after a poor night, not as a way to need less of it.

## How it differs from meditation

Many forms of meditation ask you to sit up and hold your attention on something, like your breath, and to bring it back each time it wanders. That takes some effort, and many beginners find it frustrating.

NSDR asks for less. You lie down, the voice does the steering, and the aim is rest rather than concentration. If your mind wanders, you simply come back to the next instruction. If you fall asleep, that is fine too, especially in the evening.

## What the evidence shows, and what it doesn't

The research on yoga nidra is growing and encouraging, but much of it is still small or of modest quality. A 2025 review that pooled 73 studies with over 5,000 people found improvements in stress, anxiety and low mood. The authors were clear that the studies were often weak, and that the true effects are probably smaller than the numbers suggest. A review of six randomised trials on sleep found improvements in how quickly people fell asleep and how well they slept, again with a warning about study quality.

One of the better trials is reassuring for short sessions. Adults who practised an 11-minute online yoga nidra, ideally every day for two months, had small improvements in well-being compared with people on a waiting list.

So the honest summary: NSDR is a low-cost, low-risk way to wind down that may help stress and sleep. The effects are modest, and it works best as a regular habit, not a one-off fix.

## When to use it

- In the evening, as your 10 minutes to wind down.
- After a poor night, as a midday reset.
- When you feel wired but tired.
- After a demanding day, physically or mentally.

To start, try the 10-minute guided NSDR from Huberman Lab on YouTube: [10-minute NSDR](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KHIbgSN2qAU). Start it, then turn your phone face down, so the screen does not pull you back in. Headphones help.

## What to do

Your habit: **10 minutes to wind down: box breathing or NSDR**.

- Box breathing: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4.
- Or a guided NSDR, lying down.
- Lights low.

Do it lying somewhere safe and comfortable, never while driving. If lying still with your thoughts brings up something distressing, open your eyes and stop; it is fine to choose box breathing instead.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- Falling asleep faster on the evenings they use it.
- A calmer mind, with less of the day replaying.
- Better recovery: waking a little more rested, or feeling restored after a midday session.

**NSDR is ten minutes of guided deep rest that helps you wind down; let it lead you into sleep, never replace it.**
