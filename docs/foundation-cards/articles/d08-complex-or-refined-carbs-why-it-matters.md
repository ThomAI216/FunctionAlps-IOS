Choosing whole, fibre-rich carbohydrates over refined ones gives you a steadier energy curve and can soften the afternoon dip.

Carbohydrates are not the enemy. They are your body's main fuel, especially for your brain and your muscles when you move. The question is not whether to eat them, but which kind. Two lunches with the same amount of carbohydrate can leave you feeling very different by three o'clock.

## Complex and refined: what's the difference?

**Complex carbohydrates** come in foods that are close to how they grew: oats, wholegrain bread, brown rice, quinoa, beans, lentils, whole fruit. The starch or sugar is packed in with fibre, and often with protein, vitamins and minerals.

**Refined carbohydrates** have had most of that removed. White bread, white pasta, many breakfast cereals, pastries, sweets and fruit juice are examples. The grain has lost its outer layers, or the fruit has lost its pulp, and much of the fibre has gone with them.

The difference is not the carbohydrate itself. It is everything that comes with it.

## Fibre slows things down

Fibre is the part of plant food your body can't fully digest. It slows down how quickly the carbohydrate is broken down and absorbed. That means a gentler rise in blood sugar after the meal, rather than a quick peak and a faster drop.

Fibre also feeds the bacteria in your gut and adds bulk, which helps keep digestion regular. And it matters over the long term. A large series of reviews, covering more than 180 long-term studies, found that people who ate the most fibre had lower rates of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer than those who ate the least. Those studies show a link rather than proof, but the picture is consistent, and wholegrain foods show a similar pattern.

## The energy curve and the afternoon dip

Many people know the feeling: a heavy lunch, then a slump in the middle of the afternoon, and a reach for coffee or something sweet.

The afternoon dip has more than one cause. Your body clock has a natural lull in the early afternoon, and a short night makes it worse. But lunch can make it deeper or softer. A meal built on refined carbohydrates tends to give a faster rise and fall in blood sugar. A meal built on complex carbohydrates, with protein and vegetables, tends to give a flatter, longer curve. Many people find their afternoon steadier that way. A short walk after the meal helps too, as you will see in **The walk after every meal**.

## Simple swaps

You don't need to give anything up. Swap one thing at a time:

- White bread → wholegrain or rye bread.
- White pasta and rice → wholegrain pasta, brown rice or quinoa.
- Sugary cereal → oats, porridge or muesli without added sugar.
- Fruit juice → a piece of whole fruit.
- Add beans, lentils or chickpeas to soups, salads and sauces.

## What to do

- Oats, wholegrain bread, beans, quinoa, brown rice
- White bread and pasta → wholegrain
- Whole fruit rather than juice

If you are not used to much fibre, increase it over a week or two and drink enough water, to avoid bloating. If you have a gut condition, or take medication for diabetes, ask your doctor or nutritionist before making big changes.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- Steadier energy through the day.
- No three o'clock dip, or a smaller one.
- Better digestion, with the fibre.

**Keep the carbohydrates, change the kind: whole and fibre-rich over refined.**
