*One minute each evening to rate five markers turns how your days feel into curves you can actually see.*

Your body doesn't respond the same way every day. The same lunch, the same walk, the same night can feel completely different depending on stress, sleep and what came before. Without feedback, a plan is a theory. With feedback, it becomes yours.

## The five markers

Each evening you move five sliders:

1. **Energy.** How much you had today, and whether it held up.
2. **Sleep.** How well you slept last night, and how rested you felt.
3. **Mood.** Your general state of mind: flat, irritable, steady or bright.
4. **Calmness.** How settled or tense you felt through the day.
5. **Digestion.** Comfort after meals: lightness, bloating, heaviness.

The question behind each one is simple: did today give you energy, cost you energy, or keep things steady?

## Why the evening, and why every day

Rating the day while it is still fresh is more accurate than trying to remember a whole week later. Researchers who study daily life use this same idea: short, repeated ratings close to the moment reduce the blur of memory.

Doing it around the same time each evening makes the ratings comparable, and it helps the check-in become a habit rather than a task. It takes about a minute.

And being honest matters more than being positive. A low score is not a failure; it is information. It is often the most useful entry of the week.

## How your curves become your Trends

After three days in a row, your curves appear in Trends. From there, the picture grows each evening.

Look for trends, not single days. One poor night tells us little. A week of steadier energy since you started walking after lunch tells us a lot. Over the 14 days, your curves show which habits make a difference for you, and they give us something concrete to look at together at your review.

Tracking is meant to make you clearer, not anxious. If it ever starts to feel like a chore, keep it short and simply carry on.

## What to do

**Do your functional check-in**
- Five sliders: energy, sleep, mood, calmness, digestion.
- Around the same time each evening.
- Honest beats perfect.

## What to look for

Many people notice that:

- they start to see their own patterns;
- their curves appear in Trends;
- over time, it shows what their habits change.

**One honest minute each evening turns how you feel into something you can see, and act on.**
