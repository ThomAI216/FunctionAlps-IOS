The app gives everyone the same strong foundations; a personal programme starts from you, with a plan made for you, labs where they are useful, and follow-up with Thomas.

Two weeks ago you started from where you were. Since then you have logged meals, checked in each evening, walked, breathed, moved and slept a little differently. You have chosen the habits you want to keep. Whatever you decide next, that work is yours, and it doesn't stop today.

This article is about one option for what comes after: a personal programme. It is not the only good path, and we want to be clear about what it adds, and what it doesn't.

## What the app does well

The Foundation Track is the same for everyone, on purpose. Daylight, movement, meals with protein and fibre, a calmer evening, a moment to breathe: these basics help almost everyone, and most people feel a difference from them alone. Your check-ins and meal photos have already started to show *your* patterns in Trends.

If that is enough for you right now, you can simply keep going with the app. Keep your habits, keep checking in, and keep reading. That is a complete choice, not a lesser one.

## What a personal programme adds

What the app can't do is look at you as a whole person and decide what *you* need next. That is where a personal programme comes in.

- **A plan made for you.** Not the general basics, but the few changes that matter most for your body, your goals and your life: your food, your movement, your sleep, your stress, in an order that makes sense.
- **Labs, where they are useful.** Sometimes the most helpful next step is to measure rather than guess. When tests would genuinely change the plan, Thomas will suggest them and explain why. When they wouldn't, he won't.
- **A programme built on your own data.** Your check-ins, your meal photos, your history and any results, read together, so the plan follows what is actually happening for you rather than what happens on average.
- **Follow-up with Thomas.** Regular check-ins with a practitioner who knows your story, to adjust as you go: what's working, what isn't, what to change next.

A good plan works like recovery after training: your body can only rebuild with the right materials, in the right amounts, at the right time. A personal programme is about finding *your* right materials, rather than the general ones.

A programme doesn't replace your doctor, and it doesn't promise a particular result. What it offers is attention: someone looking closely at your patterns and helping you act on them.

## The 20-minute review call

To help you decide, you can book a 20-minute review call with Thomas. It is a conversation, not a sales pitch. Together you look at your two weeks: what you noticed, which habits you kept, and what your check-ins show. You can talk about what you would like to change next, and Thomas will tell you honestly whether a personal programme would help, or whether the app is enough for now.

There is no obligation either way. Plenty of people take the call and carry on with the app, with a clearer idea of where to focus.

## What to do

- **Look back at your two weeks** in Trends, and note one or two things you'd like to understand better.
- **Book your 20-minute review call with Thomas**, if you'd like to talk it through.
- **Bring your questions**: what you noticed, what you kept, what you still want to change.
- **Or simply keep going** with the habits you chose. The app stays with you.

## What to look for

A personal programme may be worth a conversation if you notice:

- **Something that hasn't shifted**, even with the basics in place: energy, sleep, digestion or mood.
- **Questions the app can't answer**, such as *why* your body responds the way it does.
- **A wish for guidance**, someone to help you choose the next step and adjust it with you.

If none of these apply, that is good news too: your foundations are doing their job.

**Whatever you choose, the foundations you built in these two weeks are yours to keep; a personal programme simply builds the next floor around you.**
