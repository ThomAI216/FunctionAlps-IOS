You started with one 5-minute walk after eating; now it becomes a short walk after every meal.

On day 2 you added **A 5-minute walk after eating**. For many people it is one of the habits they notice first: a lighter stomach, less of the post-lunch slump. In week two, the same idea grows a little. Not a longer walk, but more of them: a few minutes after breakfast, lunch and dinner.

## Why after the meal

After you eat, your body breaks the carbohydrate in your meal down into glucose, which enters your blood. Working muscles take up glucose from the blood. So when you walk after a meal, your legs help use some of that energy, and the rise in blood sugar tends to be gentler.

You don't need to walk far or fast for this. A review of one-day studies found that breaking up sitting with short bouts of light walking lowered the rise in blood sugar and insulin after meals, compared with sitting still. The evidence is strongest for blood sugar. For digestion and comfort, your own experience is the best guide.

## Why every meal

Timing seems to matter. In one study of adults with type 2 diabetes, people were asked either to walk 30 minutes once a day, or 10 minutes after each main meal. The total was the same. The walks after meals lowered blood sugar after eating more, and the difference was largest after the evening meal, when people ate the most carbohydrate and tended to sit the longest.

That study was in people with diabetes, so we don't claim the same numbers for everyone. But the principle is simple and safe: move a little after each time you eat.

## Making it fit

- After breakfast: a few minutes on the way to work, or around the house.
- After lunch: around the block, or the long way back to your desk.
- After dinner: a gentle stroll. It fits with **The 3-2-1 rule**, since a walk is fine before bed.

## What to do

- 5 to 10 minutes
- Right after eating, or within half an hour
- Even around the house

Keep the pace easy. If you feel discomfort walking straight after a big meal, wait a little longer or slow down.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- Steadier energy all day.
- Easier digestion.
- More steps without trying.

**A few easy minutes on your feet, after every meal.**
