A palm-sized portion of protein at each meal gives your body building material, keeps you fuller and helps protect your muscle as you age.

Week two is about building your plate. We start with protein, because it is the part of the meal that most often goes missing. Many people eat most of their protein at dinner, while breakfast is toast, cereal or a coffee on the way out. Spreading it across the day is one of the simplest changes you can make.

## Your body's building material

When you eat protein, your body breaks it down into amino acids. These are used to repair and build muscle, but also to make enzymes, connective tissue, immune proteins and much more. Your body doesn't store protein the way it stores energy, so it relies on a regular supply.

A simple way to think about it: food gives you fuel and building blocks. Carbohydrates and fats are mostly fuel. Protein is mostly building blocks. Training and daily movement send the signal to repair and strengthen. Protein supplies the material for the response.

## Fuller for longer

Protein tends to be more filling than the same amount of energy from carbohydrate or fat. Reviews of feeding studies find a modest effect: people feel fuller and their fullness hormones rise after a higher-protein meal. It is not magic, and it won't override everything else you eat. But a breakfast with eggs or yogurt often carries people through to lunch better than one made only of bread or cereal, and that can mean fewer cravings mid-morning.

## Muscle, as we age

From middle age onwards, muscle mass and strength tend to decline slowly, and in later life that loss can affect balance, energy and independence. Movement is the main signal to keep muscle. Enough protein is what lets your body act on that signal. Expert groups on ageing advise that older adults generally need more protein than younger adults, not less.

How you spread it may matter too. In one small study, adults who ate a similar amount of protein at breakfast, lunch and dinner built more muscle protein over the day than when most of it came at dinner. It was a small study, so we take it as a pointer rather than a rule, but it supports the simple idea of some protein at every meal.

## Easy sources, meal by meal

You don't need to weigh anything. Use your hand: a portion about the size and thickness of your palm.

- **Breakfast**: eggs, Greek yogurt, quark or cottage cheese, smoked salmon, a tofu scramble.
- **Lunch**: chicken, tinned fish such as sardines or tuna, lentils, chickpeas or beans, tofu, a couple of eggs on a salad.
- **Dinner**: fish, chicken, beans or lentils, tofu, eggs.

Plant sources count. Beans and lentils also bring fibre, which you will read about in **Complex or refined carbs: why it matters**. If you eat mostly plants, mixing sources across the day helps.

## What to do

- Eggs, yogurt, fish, chicken, tofu, beans or lentils
- About the size of your palm
- Start with breakfast

If you have kidney disease, or have been told to watch your protein, ask your doctor or nutritionist before changing how much you eat.

## What to look for

Many people notice:

- Feeling fuller for longer after meals.
- Fewer cravings, especially mid-morning and late afternoon.
- Over the long run, the benefit you can't feel day to day: keeping your muscle as you age.

**A palm of protein at every meal, starting with breakfast.**
