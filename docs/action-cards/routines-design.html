Routines and Evolutions

How weekly priorities become actions, how actions live in a morning, day and evening routine, how each action evolves into the next, and the rules that stop anyone burning out. Everything uses cards we already have; the few levels still to write are tagged new.

Brainstorm. No database change until the design settles.
The process

One cycle, every 1 or 2 weeks

The autopilot runs this loop for trial and free members from their track. A clinician runs it in CLINICAL for members in care.

  1. ObjectiveThe member picks energy, sleep, focus, brain fog, muscle, weight or stress. That picks a track; the Basic track runs for everyone.
  2. Priorities1 to 3 for the cycle, for example same bedtime, no caffeine after 14:00, screens off 30 min before bed.
  3. PlacementEach priority becomes an action in the morning, day or evening routine, within the caps.
  4. DailyThe member runs the routines. Each card still flexes gentle / standard / further with the day's readiness.
  5. EvolutionDone enough times at its level, an action offers its next level.
  6. ReviewCompletion, streaks, level-ups, priorities kept. Then keep, level up, swap, or take the next block.
The routines

What goes in a day

Three actions in the morning, at most six spread over the day routines, three in the evening. A new member starts at two, two and two.

Morning

max 3

From waking to the first work block

  • HydrateA glass of water before coffee
  • LightMorning light within an hour of waking
  • MoveMorning stretch, five minutesclimbs the morning-movement ladder: stretch → + circuit or 10-min walk → 20–40-min walk → easy run

Swaps: ten focused breaths on waking · protein at breakfast · first coffee 30 min after waking · same wake-up time, weekends too (the routine's start time)

During the day

max 6 in all

Routines that switch on when they apply: Sport only on training days

Work focus blocks

  • Phone in another room while you focus
  • Single-task the first work hour
  • A two-minute pause between tasks
  • The same small ritual at each switch

Energy reset the dip

  • A 10-minute walk after lunch
  • Five minutes outside in daylight
  • The double-inhale sigh
  • Ten minutes of guided deep rest
  • A short nap Rx

Sport training days

  • Warm-up (morning stretch)
  • The session: strength or cardio Rx
  • Full attention on every rep
  • A proper meal after training
  • Hard workouts end 3–4 h before bed

Meals main meals

  • Vegetables on half the plate
  • Protein first, then the rest
  • Slow first five bites
  • Three slow breaths before meals

Evening

max 3

The last one to three hours before bed

  • ScreensScreens off 30 minutes before bedladder: phone out of the bedroom → 30 min → 60 min
  • LightDim the lights after 21:00
  • Wind downFive minutes of cyclic sighingor tomorrow's list · a mental walk at lights-out · slow eye movements

Swaps: a cool bedroom · a hot shower before bed · kitchen closed after 21:00 · same bedtime on weekdays (the routine's end time)

Priorities on top

This cycle's one to three priorities come first, starred, with how many days they held this week. Everything else is the routines the member already runs.

Routines as stacks

Morning, the day routines that apply today, evening. "Start routine" walks through one action at a time. A full routine says so and offers a swap rather than a fourth action.

The ladder on every action

The sheet shows each level, where the member stands, "2 of 3 to unlock the next level", and when each level was reached. Today's gentle / standard / further version is separate: it moves with the day, the level moves with the weeks.

Streaks and progress

Days in a row per action and per routine, the best streak kept, one grace day a week, a completion calendar, and a timeline of level-ups. A missed day never shows as "lost".

Evolutions

Every action is a step on a ladder

Each level is a card. A level can offer a choice ("or"). The first two ladders are yours; the others are built from the cards we have. Levels from the book keep the book's numbers.

The rules

Guardrails, with numbers to settle

Every number below is a proposal. A clinician can override them for one member; the practice sets the defaults.

RuleProposalWhy
Caps per routine3 · 6 · 3
new member 2 · 2 · 2		Morning, day, evening. Nobody gets twelve actions on day one.
Actions a member adds alonetrial ≤ 3 · member ≤ 6
1 new per 3 days		The bank is a temptation; the limit protects the routine. Prescribed actions don't count here, but they count in the caps.
Level up3 done in 7 days
2–3×/week cards: 2 weeks		Your "two or three times first". The app offers the next level; the member accepts or stays. A clinician can lock a level or set 2.
One change at a time1 level-up per routine per weekA routine changes one thing at a time so it stays recognisable.
Stack on a stable baseroutine ≥ 70 % over 7 daysA new action joins a routine only when the routine already holds. Otherwise the app suggests a gentler level or a swap.
Step down, never punish3 misses in a row → offerThe app offers the level below or a pause. Never automatic, never "streak lost".
Streaks1 grace day per 7Per action and per routine; weekly cards count weeks; the best streak is kept.
Priorities1–3 per cycle
cycle = 1 or 2 weeks		Starred, shown first, counted in the review.
Tierstrial · members · RxTrial cards in the 14-day trial, members-only cards for paying members, prescription cards placed by a clinician only.
Who winsclinician > memberA member can pause a prescribed action for today; removing it asks the clinician.

These rules read completions only, never health data: the doctrine of the existing habit loop (lib/habit-loop/arithmetic.ts: "gates read behaviour, not health"). The level-up rule is an existing gate with required_n = 3 and window_days = 7; the streak arithmetic already exists too.
